Report of Referee B -- DS13372/Zeeshan (New Referee)
----------------------------------------------------------------------

I have read the latest version of the paper as well as the previous
correspondence between the authors and the referee.

I don't understand the first point in the authors' latest reply. They
assume that analyzing an eccentric binary with a quasi-circular
template effectively absorbs the eccentricity into a redefinition of
the chirp mass via Equation 16. When computing the error according to
this assumption, one should substitute Mecc into McT in Equation 12.
This implies that the uncertain value of Mc depends on the
eccentricity. This seems like an inconsistent assumption, as pointed
out by the previous referee. If this is not the case, I urge the
authors to clarify this point very clearly in the draft.


While reading the draft, I noted the following points for improvement:

- The degeneracy between spin precession (here neglected) and
eccentricity can be crucial (e.g., arxiv:2211.07528). This is an
important caveat to this study.

- Please run a grammar check; some sentences do not flow well.

- Please define sigma_e in both the abstract and the introduction.

- Sections 2.A-B-C can be greatly shortened. Only a few technical
details are necessary; repeating the formalism is not.

- Why use a one-sided Gaussian for the eccentricity? Is this motivated
by some astrophysical models?

- The assumption that VT does not depend on the eccentricity is a
strong one, with potentially disruptive consequences for the paper's
main conclusions. It is well known that Bayesian population fits are
very sensitive to the treatment of selection effects. Please comment
on this point. The authors could easily implement an SNR cut using
eccentric waveforms. Why are they taking a shortcut here?

- More details are needed on how the errors on the eccentricity were
chosen.

- Please normalize the histograms in Figure 5.
