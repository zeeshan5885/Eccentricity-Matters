Response to the second submission:

Major concern 1: I do not agree with the comment that the study makes any sense
without defining eccentricity at some reference frequency. The eccentric chirp
mass definition (eqn 16) is only valid for very small eccentricities defined at
10 Hz. So errors on the eccentric chirp mass need to be defined at 10 Hz else
the errors and relative chirp mass shifts will be misleading. For example, let
us assume that the eccentricities are measured at 40 Hz but the distribution
(\sigma_e = 0.15) is defined at 10 Hz. In this case, it is very unlikely that we
will measure any eccentricity at 40 Hz and by then the binary would have been
circularized. On the other hand, if the eccentricities are defined at 40 Hz, the
definition of eccentric chirp mass will not make any sense and it will be
invalid as at 10 Hz eccentricities would have been too large.

Hence one needs to correctly account for reference frequency when determining
errors in parameter measurements for this study to be astrophysical relevant.
This should apply to the statement in the abstract as well: "Our analysis
demonstrates that eccentric population inference can identify the signatures of
even modest eccentricity distribution defined at reference frequency f_0."

I think the inconsistencies in the semi-analytical chirp mass - eccentricity
(via eccentric chirp mass) error modelling need to be a least clearly stated
with the inclusion reference frequencies if not completely rectified by evolving
eccentricities or recalibrating chirp mass errors at 10 Hz.


Major concern 2 is well addressed by the new paragraph on page 5.
