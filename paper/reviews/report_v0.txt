In this work, the authors try to understand the effect of including
eccentricity in the population analysis of BBH mergers. They show that
even small signatures of the presence of eccentricity can be
identified. The method and work is good but there are a couple of
major concerns that need to be addressed before publishing of the
manuscript.

Major concerns:

1. The reference frequency (f_0) for this work is noted as 20 Hz in
the text between equations 13 and 14. As per the ref 52, eqn 16 valid
for f_0 = 10. There is no mention of eccentricity evolution as a
function of frequency that is used to estimate the correct eccentric
chirp mass at 10 Hz (for example, see Fig 5 of
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.07892). Then one needs to examine the
validity of the eqn 15 for the sigma_ecc = 0.15 case.

2. For total mass in the range of 20-100 solar masses, merger-ringdown
part contributes significantly. Eqn 16 is mainly valid in the
inspiral-dominated region of the mass space. This usually implies that
the errors in the masses are underestimated. Comments on this are
warranted.

Then there are a few minor comments:
1. By contrast, for low-mass binaries, previous studies have
demonstrated that eccentricities can be particularly well- constrained
(for low-mass objects) [51], owing to their long modulated inspiral
[52], even it improves the accuracy in parameter inference [53].
Repetition to be removed.

2. To extend our analysis, we compared constrained parameters using
the EBBH and CBBH, which …. It would be good to explain E/CBBH here
even though it seems obvious.

3. We use the likelihood provided in Eq. 2 in the Bayes theorem
defined in Eq. 3 to find … (Eq 1 instead of 3 here)

4. Horizon distance for a given event changes (increases) with the
inclusion of eccentricity. How was that accounted for?
